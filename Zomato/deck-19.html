Understand

Ordering food is a trust problem before it is a hunger problem

Hesitation points

  • Will the final bill match the menu prices?
  • Will it arrive when promised?
  • Did my payment go through?

What the flow must do

  • Show every charge before payment
  • Keep status visible after payment
  • Let people undo and recover at each step

Scope of this study

  • Mobile customer flow only
  • Login, search, menu, cart and bill, payment, tracking
  • Sample data; no real restaurants

Understand

One flow, six screens, one question: where would a first-time user hesitate?

Design goals

  • Fewer taps from login to a confirmed order
  • A bill with no surprises
  • Honest, always-visible order status

Out of scope

  • Restaurant-side tools
  • Delivery partner app
  • Real payments, maps or live data

Evidence level

This is a design exercise on a clickable prototype. Statements here are design reasoning and proposed tests, not findings from the live Zomato app.

Understand

Ananya, 27, orders dinner on her phone after work

Who

Product analyst in a metro city. Orders 2 to 3 times a week, mostly on a commute home. One-handed use, patchy network.

Goal

Pick something familiar quickly, pay without second-guessing, and know when it will arrive.

Frustrations

  • Fees that appear only at checkout
  • Cart that empties when switching restaurant
  • Status that stops updating

Solve

Make the first screen impossible to fail

Decision

  • Phone number first, no password
  • OTP as four separate boxes with auto-advance
  • Backspace moves to the previous box

Heuristic

Error prevention. The button validates the number before sending, and a wrong OTP says what is wrong and how to fix it.

To test

Does the demo hint make the OTP step feel safe, or does it look like a bug?

Solve

Narrow choices with chips, and let a dish name find its restaurant

Decision

  • Search matches restaurants and dishes
  • Cuisine chips replace a long filter sheet
  • Veg-only toggle always visible

Law applied

Hick's Law. Fewer visible choices at once shortens decision time. Chips show five options, not fifty.

To test

Do people search by dish or by cuisine first? Does the dish match line under a result help?

Solve

Make adding an item a single, forgiving tap

Decision

  • ADD turns into a quantity stepper in place
  • Veg and non-veg markers on every item
  • Sticky cart bar with live item total

Law applied

Fitts's Law. Steppers are 34px square or larger, with the bar fixed at thumb height.

To test

Is the sticky bar noticed? Do people trust the item total before the bill?

Solve

Show the whole bill before payment, with the rule behind each fee

Bill lines

  • Item total and coupon discount
  • Delivery fee, free from 399 rupees
  • Platform fee and packing
  • GST and taxes, optional tip
  • To pay

Decision

  • Free-delivery progress shown as a line
  • Coupon with a visible minimum
  • Tip chosen before payment, not after

Heuristic

Visibility of system status. The price the user sees at checkout equals the price they pay.

Solve

Four methods, validated before the button works

Decision

  • UPI, card, wallet, cash on delivery
  • Selected method shown with a filled dot
  • Total repeated on the button

Error handling

  • Invalid UPI ID or short card number blocks submit
  • Message names the field to fix

To test

Is cash on delivery easy to find without distracting from online payment?

Solve

Silence after payment is the worst moment, so never go quiet

Decision

  • Order ID and summary at the top
  • Four stages: placed, preparing, out for delivery, delivered
  • ETA counts down; rider moves on the map

Law applied

Peak-End Rule. The last screen shapes memory of the whole order, so it carries the most care.

To test

Do people check the map or the timeline first? Is the call button findable?

Solve

Built for one thumb, a small screen and a bad network

Mobile-first

  • Single column, max width 430px
  • Sticky bottom actions
  • No hover-only controls

Accessibility

  • Text and controls follow the system light or dark theme
  • Visible focus on inputs
  • Colour is never the only signal (veg markers have shape)

Known gaps

  • No screen-reader audit yet
  • No offline or slow-network state
  • English only

Prove

How to find out whether the design works

Metrics

  • Order completion: login to confirmed
  • Drop-off by step
  • Time to first item added
  • Payment failure rate

Test plan

  • 5 to 6 participants, moderated, think-aloud
  • Task 1: find and order a veg dish
  • Task 2: explain the final bill
  • Task 3: find the order status

Success looks like

Most participants can state the total before paying and the order stage after paying, without help.
